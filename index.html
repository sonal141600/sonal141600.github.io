Connecting Rod Modelling

Co-authored paper in Life Cycle Reliability and Safety Engineering. I wrote the mathematical model that sizes the rod from engine specifications; the team then ran ANSYS finite element analysis on a structural steel rod for stress and deformation.

Read the abstract

The connecting rod links the piston to the crankshaft and turns the piston's reciprocating motion into rotation. This study first used mathematical modelling to calculate the rod's dimensions and the forces it must carry, based on engine specifications. A 3D model was then meshed with tetrahedral elements in ANSYS 2024 R1 and analysed for total deformation, von Mises stress, and normal and shear stress along the X, Y and Z axes. The analysis identified the regions of highest stress concentration and maximum deformation, which can guide optimized designs for better engine efficiency, reliability and performance.

V. Vishal Asrith · Shreyash Mann · Sonal Garg · Jiyaul Mustafa

Precision XY Alignment Stage

Centers the specimen in a Split Hopkinson Pressure Bar, the rig used for high strain-rate material testing. If the specimen sits even slightly off the bar axis, the impact load bends it and the test data is no longer valid.

I designed a four-part, box-in-box mechanism in aluminium 7075/6061: a fixed base, a Y-axis sled, a nested X-axis double sled that holds the specimen, and a handle that drives a fine-pitch lead screw. Each axis adjusts independently. I documented it as a full manufacturing package with drawings and dimension tables for every part.
